1. Introduction and Motivation
Large Language Models (LLMs) and their smaller variants (SLMs, roughly 1–3B parameters) frequently 
produce fluent but factually wrong or ungrounded text — a failure called hallucination. A medical query 
bot that invents a drug dose, a summarizer that adds facts absent from the source, or a math tutor that 
silently corrupts one arithmetic step can all cause real harm. Two large 2026 studies frame the problem for 
our setting precisely: (i) smaller models hallucinate significantly more than larger ones, and (ii) 
hallucination is worse and harder to measure in low-resource, non-English languages, where uncertainty 
signals are often mis-calibrated.
Bengali is the sixth most spoken language in the world, yet until mid-2026 no framework even measured 
hallucination in Bengali LLMs. BenHalluEval (2026) closed that measurement gap for four tasks 
(Generative QA, Bangla–English code-mixed QA, summarization, reasoning) and showed two 
uncomfortable facts: hallucination calibration varies wildly across models, and chain-of-thought 
prompting — the usual cheap fix — helped in only 9 of 21 model–task combinations and hurt in 12.
The open problem this thesis attacks: Bengali has an evaluation benchmark but no mitigation framework. 
BenHalluEval explicitly lists “consistency-based mitigation strategies” as future work. The only Bengali 
self-correction work (BanglaForge, 2025) is restricted to code generation, and notes that Bengali 
generation systems still lack built-in grounding, correction, or iteration mechanisms. Reducing — not just 
detecting — hallucination in Bengali SLMs is unexplored territory.
2. Why This Is Method-Level Novelty (not a dataset swap)
A common weak thesis pattern is: take an existing English method, run it on a Bengali dataset, report a 
higher accuracy. That is replication, not contribution. SETU is different in mechanism, and the contribution 
survives even if datasets change:
• A type-aware triage router. Every existing mitigation method applies a single uniform fix — either 
retrieval, or self-verification. SETU first classifies each hallucination-prone claim as data-driven 
(missing knowledge) or reasoning-driven (logical error), then routes it to the correct repair. No 
prior Bengali or SLM system does this routing.
• A first calibration study of uncertainty signals for Bengali SLMs. Multilingual studies report that 
uncertainty is mis-calibrated in low-resource languages, but no one has measured which signal 
(semantic entropy, self-consistency, verbalized confidence) is actually reliable in Bengali. This is a 
dataset-independent scientific finding.

• Mitigation, not detection. The entire Bengali hallucination literature to date is 
detection/evaluation only. SETU produces corrected or abstained answers and is scored on how 
much it lowers hallucination.
• Abstention as a first-class output. When neither repair resolves a flagged claim, SETU makes the 
model say “
 
 ” 
আমিনিশ্চিতনা(I am not sure) instead of committing a confident error — aligned with the 
2026 shift from chasing zero hallucination to managing uncertainty.
• Code-mixed mitigation. Bangla–English code-mixed text is heavily used online and is a distinct task 
in BenHalluEval, but mitigation on code-mixed input is completely unexamined.
Proof of contribution: an ablation study turns each SETU component off in isolation, so the improvement 
is demonstrably in the mechanism, not in data selection.
3. Research Questions
1. RQ1. Which uncertainty signals are calibrated enough to flag hallucination-prone claims in Bengali 
SLMs, and does calibration differ between native Bengali and code-mixed input?
2. RQ2. Can hallucinations be reliably triaged as data-driven vs reasoning-driven in a black-box SLM 
setting without model-internal access?
3. RQ3. Does type-aware routing (retrieval for data-driven, belief-consistency for reasoning-driven) 
reduce hallucination more than a single uniform fix, measured by dual-track BenHalluScore?
4. RQ4. Does uncertainty-grounded abstention improve overall reliability without excessively 
refusing answerable questions?

4. Base Papers (verified, 2024–2026)
SETU stands on six primary papers. Each contributes one reusable component; SETU’s novelty is the 
combination and the routing between them.
4.1 BenHalluEval — arXiv 2605.31483 (updated Jun 2026) [evaluation protocol]
• What it gives us: the dual-track evaluation protocol (Track A on ground-truth answers to catch 
false alarms; Track B on hallucinated candidates to catch misses) and the BenHalluScore metric = 
½×(Track A error + Track B error). Also the four Bengali tasks and 12,000 hallucinated candidates.
• Gap we exploit: detection/evaluation only; CoT mitigation is unreliable (9/21 improve, 12/21 
degrade); mitigation is left as future work.
4.2 HalluGuard — ICLR 2026 [triage inspiration]
• What it gives us: the formal decomposition of hallucination into a data-driven term 
(flawed/missing knowledge) and a reasoning-driven term (inference-time instability). SETU 
borrows this taxonomy as the basis of its triage router.
• Gap we exploit: HalluGuard is a detection score requiring NTK/internal access on English 
benchmarks; SETU makes the data-vs-reasoning decision black-box and lightweight, then acts on it 
to correct output.
4.3 BTPROP (Belief Tree Propagation) — NAACL 2025 [reasoning-driven fix]
• What it gives us: a consistency-based verification idea — expand a claim into logically related 
statements and use NLI + confidence consistency to judge truthfulness. SETU uses a lightweight 
version as the reasoning-driven repair.
• Gap we exploit: English-only, and its full belief tree is compute-heavy; SETU applies a shallow, 
SLM-friendly consistency check triggered only for reasoning-flagged claims.
4.4 HalluSearch — SemEval-2025 Task 3 (Mu-SHROOM) [data-driven fix]
• What it gives us: atomic factual-claim splitting + retrieval-augmented verification, with fallback 
strategies when search coverage is thin. SETU adopts claim splitting and retrieval verification for 
data-driven claims.
• Gap we exploit: it reports degraded performance in low-online-coverage languages; SETU adds a 
cross-lingual (Bengali→English) retrieval fallback to compensate for the sparse Bengali corpus.
4.5 “How Much Do LLMs Hallucinate across Languages?” (MFAVA) — arXiv 2502.12769 (updated Feb 
2026) [motivation]
• What it gives us: empirical grounding — smaller models hallucinate more; broader-language 
models hallucinate more; hallucination rate does not simply track a language’s data size; and 
hallucination-rate estimation must correct for detector precision/recall. Justifies focusing on SLMs 
and on careful metric design.

4.6 Cost-Efficient Cross-Lingual RAG for Bengali — arXiv 2601.02065 (Jan 2026) [cross-lingual 
grounding]
• What it gives us: evidence that translating Bengali queries to English, retrieving from a high-
resource corpus, and returning in Bengali improves low-resource grounding. SETU turns this static 
pipeline into an adaptive fallback used only when the Bengali corpus is insufficient.

7. Methodology — The SETU Pipeline
7.1 High-level flow
Bengali (or code-mixed) query → SLM draft answer → (1) atomic claim decomposition → (2) per-claim 
uncertainty scoring → flag high-uncertainty claims → (3) triage each flagged claim as data-driven or 
reasoning-driven → (4) targeted correction: cross-lingual retrieval for data-driven, belief-consistency 
check for reasoning-driven → (5) if unresolved, abstain on that claim → reassemble a corrected, grounded 
answer.
7.2 Stage-by-stage
1. Draft generation. A Bengali-capable SLM (candidates: Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2B, 
and a Bangla-centric model in the TigerLLM line) produces an initial answer at 4-bit precision.
2. Atomic claim decomposition (Bengali-adapted). Split the answer into single-fact claims (following 
the HalluSearch / FActScore idea), so each claim can be verified independently. Prompt templates 
are authored and validated by native Bengali speakers on the team.
3. Per-claim uncertainty scoring (RQ1). Compute three signals per claim — semantic entropy 
(multiple samples clustered by NLI), self-consistency (agreement across samples), and verbalized 
confidence — and measure their calibration (Expected Calibration Error, AUROC vs gold) 
separately for native and code-mixed input. This calibration study is a standalone contribution.
4. Triage / routing (RQ2). A lightweight classifier labels each flagged claim data-driven (answer needs 
external facts — entity/date/quantity) or reasoning-driven (a logical/arithmetic step is wrong). 
Signals: retrieval-answerability probe, claim type (factual vs inferential), and self-contradiction 
under paraphrase. Grounded in HalluGuard’s decomposition but done black-box.
5. Data-driven correction. Retrieve from a Bengali Wikipedia FAISS index (embeddings: BGE-M3 / 
multilingual-e5, plus BM25). If Bengali retrieval score is below threshold, fall back to cross-lingual 
retrieval: translate the claim to English, retrieve from English Wikipedia, return grounding in 
Bengali (adaptive version of the 2026 Bengali cross-lingual RAG pipeline). Revise the claim to 
match retrieved evidence.
6. Reasoning-driven correction. Run a shallow belief-consistency check (lightweight BTPROP): 
generate a few logically related sub-claims, use an NLI model to test entailment/contradiction, and 
revise the claim if it is inconsistent with its own supported premises.
7. Abstention (RQ4). If a flagged claim remains unresolved after correction, replace it with an explicit 
“
 
 ” 
আমিনিশ্চিতনাmarker rather than emitting a confident guess.
8. Reassembly. Recombine verified, corrected, and abstained claims into the final Bengali answer.

7.4 Baselines and evaluation
• Baselines: (a) raw SLM, no mitigation; (b) chain-of-thought prompting (BenHalluEval’s tested fix); 
(c) uniform RAG (retrieve-then-answer, no triage); (d) uniform self-verification (chain-of-
verification). SETU must beat all four.
• Primary metric: dual-track BenHalluScore (lower is better) on each of the four tasks — penalizes 
both false alarms and missed hallucinations.
• Secondary metrics: task accuracy / F1, abstention rate and over-abstention rate, retrieval 
recall@k, and calibration (ECE / AUROC) for RQ1.
• Statistics: multiple seeds with paired significance tests between SETU and each baseline — the 
same rigorous protocol the team used in its federated-learning thesis.
7.5 Ablations (this is what proves method-level novelty)
• Remove claim decomposition (verify whole answer at once).
• Remove triage (apply one uniform fix to every flagged claim).
• Remove cross-lingual fallback (Bengali retrieval only).
• Remove belief-consistency (retrieval for everything).
• Remove abstention (always answer).
Each removed component should measurably raise BenHalluScore, demonstrating the gain lives in the 
routing mechanism — not in dataset choice.
7.6 Compute feasibility (Kaggle)
• All inference, no pre-training. 1.5–3B SLMs run at 4-bit on a Kaggle P100/T4; LoRA is available if 
any light fine-tuning of the triage classifier is needed.
• Uncertainty cost is bounded: semantic entropy needs ~5–10 samples per flagged claim plus a 
small NLI model — tractable because it runs only on flagged claims, not every token.
• Retrieval: BGE-M3 / multilingual-e5 embeddings + FAISS over a chunked Wikipedia subset; no API 
cost.

9. Risks and Mitigations
• Uncertainty signals may be poorly calibrated in Bengali. This is not a failure — it is RQ1’s finding 
and a publishable result. If all signals are weak, use retrieval-answerability as the primary flag.
• Sparse Bengali retrieval corpus. The cross-lingual English fallback is designed exactly for this; 
report how often it triggers.
• Triage misclassification. Report triage accuracy separately; a claim wrongly routed still gets a fix, 
so errors degrade gracefully rather than catastrophically.
• Compute pressure from multi-sample uncertainty. Sampling runs only on flagged claims; cap 
samples at 5–10 and cache NLI results.
• Citation reliability. Every reference is pulled from its original venue page; the discredited topic PDF 
is not used.
